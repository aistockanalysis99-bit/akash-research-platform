3. THE 4 GENERATIONS OF QUANTITATIVE PAIRS TRADING STRATEGIES

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.1 Strategy 1: Baseline Cointegrated Stat-Arb v1
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Design Philosophy:
  Classical textbook statistical arbitrage. When two cointegrated stocks deviate
  by 2 standard deviations from their rolling 90-day mean spread, take an equal
  dollar-weighted beta-neutral position and hold until the spread crosses the zero
  mean line (Z = 0.0).
- Key Parameters:
  * Entry Z-Score Hurdle: |Z| >= 2.00 sigma
  * Exit Target: Z = 0.00 sigma (complete mean reversion)
  * Hard Stop Loss Barrier: |Z| >= 3.50 sigma
  * Rolling Lookback Window: 90 trading days
  * Sizing: Fixed equal allocation (1.0x baseline)
- Empirical Findings:
  Strategy 1 produces positive total returns across all multi-year horizons, but
  suffers from a low win rate (~40.8% - 44.4%) and painful drawdowns of ~45% - 47%.
  Many trades drift aimlessly or take 15-20 days before hitting the hard 3.5 sigma
  stop loss because there is no time-stop mechanism.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.2 Strategy 2: Confirmed Reversion & Staged Exits (Failed Experiment Analysis)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Design Philosophy:
  Attempted to fix Strategy 1's low win rate by implementing a staged profit-taking
  model: close 50% of the trade early at Z = 0.80 sigma, move stop loss to breakeven,
  and let the remaining 50% runner trail to Z = 0.20 sigma.
- Key Parameters:
  * Entry Hurdle: |Z| >= 2.10 sigma with hook confirmation (|Z| <= 1.85)
  * First Take-Profit (TP1): Z = 0.80 sigma (50% closed)
  * Second Take-Profit (TP2): Z = 0.20 sigma (50% runner)
  * Stop Loss: 3.20 sigma (moved to breakeven after TP1)
  * Half-Life Time Stop: 1.75x half-life (min 10d, max 25d)
- Why Strategy 2 Failed (Critical Quantitative Lesson):
  While Strategy 2 achieved a high nominal win rate (~75% - 80%), its total net
  return collapsed by over 60% compared to Strategy 1 (dropping to +18% - +34%).
  By taking 50% profit early at 0.80 sigma, the strategy truncated the fat right tail
  of winning mean-reversion trades. When a trade hit a stop loss, it incurred a full
  loss on the full position size. Truncating the upside while leaving the downside
  intact destroyed the expectancy and ruined the profit factor.
  Preserved in the platform as a permanent documented negative control.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.3 Strategy 3: Asymmetric Alpha Maximizer
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Design Philosophy:
  Engineered to maximize raw net profit across 1Y to 5Y periods. Eliminated the
  costly entry hook delay of Strategy 2, allowed positions to ride 100% of the way
  to the mean line (Z = 0.05 sigma), added conviction-based capital sizing via
  ADF/cointegration composite scoring, and implemented an asymmetric ratchet
  disaster floor.
- Key Parameters:
  * Entry Hurdle: |Z| >= 2.00 sigma (zero hook delay)
  * Exit Target: Z = 0.05 sigma (full ride)
  * Overshoot Runner: Z = -0.20 sigma
  * Hard Stop Loss: 3.50 sigma
  * Conviction Sizing Tiers:
    - Tier 1 (Score >= 82, Vol <= 4.5%): 1.45x capital multiplier
    - Tier 2 (Score >= 70): 1.00x multiplier
    - Tier 3 (Score < 70): 0.60x multiplier
  * Ratchet Disaster Floor: If trade recovers past Z = 0.70 sigma, stop loss
    immediately ratchets up from 3.50 sigma to 1.50 sigma.
- Empirical Findings:
  Strategy 3 achieved massive gross profit and higher cumulative returns than
  Strategy 1 across every horizon (+84% to +161%). However, because Tier 1 trades
  carried a 1.45x multiplier, a sequence of correlated diverging pairs caused a
  catastrophic 78.05% Max Drawdown. Win rate remained low at ~40% - 42.6%.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.4 Strategy 4: Risk-Engineered Pure Alpha (Production Champion)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Design Philosophy:
  Specifically engineered to conquer the two lingering flaws of Strategy 1 and
  Strategy 3: the unacceptable 78% drawdown and the low 40% win rate.
  Strategy 4 enforces 6 institutional risk shields:
  1. Entry Velocity & Momentum Slope Filter: Prevents catching "falling knives".
     Trade triggers only if |Z| >= 2.10 sigma AND the 2-day rate of change
     |Delta Z| <= 0.05 or is actively reversing toward zero.
  2. Realistic Target Exit (Z = 0.40 sigma): Captures the steep, high-velocity 80%
     meat of the mean-reversion move without suffering the high-drag plateau that
     occurs between 0.40 and 0.00 sigma.
  3. Dynamic Tight Stop Loss (2.85 sigma / Entry + 1.15 sigma): Slashes losing

  4. Volatility-Inverse Position Sizing:
     multiplier = clamp(0.40, 3.5 / spread_vol_pct, 1.25)
     Tightly-bound spreads receive full sizing; turbulent spreads are down-scaled.
  5. Half-Life Dynamic Time Stop:
     max_days = clamp(4, round(2.5 * half_life), 22)
     Dead-money trades that fail to mean-revert within their mathematical half-life
     are liquidated immediately to redeploy capital.
  6. Macro & Event Overlay:
     - VIX panic threshold (> 25.0 freezes new entries; > 32 cuts exposure by 50%).
     - Earnings blackout: 5 days before and 2 days after quarterly earnings.
- Empirical Findings:
  Strategy 4 is the undisputed production champion. Max drawdown collapsed from
  78.05% to 3.94% (1Y), 9.56% (2Y, 3Y), and 18.99% (5Y). Win rate surged to
  68.5% (1Y), 62.5% (2Y), 58.4% (3Y), and 54.0% (5Y). Profit factor rose to 3.55 (1Y).

4. MATHEMATICAL & QUANTITATIVE FOUNDATION

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.1 Cointegration & Augmented Dickey-Fuller (ADF) Theory
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Two non-stationary price series P_A(t) and P_B(t) are integrated of order 1, I(1).
Under the Engle-Granger two-step theorem, if a linear combination:
  epsilon(t) = P_A(t) - beta * P_B(t) - alpha
is stationary, I(0), then P_A and P_B are said to be cointegrated.

The Augmented Dickey-Fuller (ADF) unit-root regression on the residual spread is:
  Delta epsilon(t) = gamma * epsilon(t-1) + sum_{i=1}^{p} phi_i * Delta epsilon(t-i) + u(t)
We test the null hypothesis H0: gamma = 0 (unit root, non-stationary) against
the alternative H1: gamma < 0 (stationary mean-reverting spread).
A pair qualifies for our universe only when:
  ADF t-statistic <= -2.85  and  p-value <= 0.045

The Ornstein-Uhlenbeck (OU) Mean Reversion Half-Life:
  d epsilon(t) = theta * (mu - epsilon(t)) * dt + sigma * dW(t)
The discrete-time regression:
  epsilon(t) - epsilon(t-1) = -lambda * epsilon(t-1) + e(t)
Yields the half-life of mean reversion:
  Half-Life = ln(2) / lambda
Only pairs with 2.0 <= Half-Life <= 24.0 trading days pass the gauntlet filter.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.2 OLS Hedge Ratio (Beta) & Spread Formulation
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Over a rolling 90-day lookback window [t-90, t], Ordinary Least Squares (OLS)
regression derives the optimal hedge ratio beta:
  P_A(t) = alpha + beta * P_B(t) + epsilon(t)
where:
  beta = Cov(P_A, P_B) / Var(P_B)
  alpha = mean(P_A) - beta * mean(P_B)

The daily spread value S(t) is then:
  S(t) = P_A(t) - beta * P_B(t) - alpha

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.3 Rolling Z-Score Formulation
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
To normalize the spread across different stock price regimes:
  mu_S = (1 / N) * sum_{k=0}^{N-1} S(t-k)    (where N = 90 days)
  sigma_S = sqrt( (1 / (N-1)) * sum_{k=0}^{N-1} (S(t-k) - mu_S)^2 )
  Z(t) = (S(t) - mu_S) / sigma_S

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.4 Position Sizing, Real-Money Execution & Leg P&L Math
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
When Z(t) <= -2.10 sigma (Spread is abnormally undervalued):
  -> Long Spread: BUY Stock A, SHORT Stock B.
When Z(t) >= +2.10 sigma (Spread is abnormally overvalued):
  -> Short Spread: SHORT Stock A, BUY Stock B.

Position Sizing on a $100,000 Portfolio:
- Baseline allocation per trade: $10,000 (10% of portfolio).
- Volatility-Inverse Multiplier (Strategy 4):
    M = clamp(0.40, 3.5 / spread_vol_pct, 1.25)
- Effective Capital Allocation:
    Capital_Trade = $10,000 * M
- Share Quantities (for 1 unit of Long Spread: Buy 1 share A, Short beta shares B):
    Unit Cost = P_A(entry) + beta * P_B(entry)
    Spread Units (U) = Capital_Trade / Unit Cost
    Qty_A = round(U)
    Qty_B = round(U * beta)

Individual Leg P&L Formulation:
For a Long A / Short B trade:
  P&L_A ($) = Qty_A * (P_A(exit) - P_A(entry))
  P&L_B ($) = Qty_B * (P_B(entry) - P_B(exit))
  Net P&L ($) = P&L_A + P&L_B
  Return (%) = Net P&L / (Qty_A * P_A(entry) + Qty_B * P_B(entry))

For a Short A / Long B trade:
  P&L_A ($) = Qty_A * (P_A(entry) - P_A(exit))
  P&L_B ($) = Qty_B * (P_B(exit) - P_B(entry))
  Net P&L ($) = P&L_A + P&L_B

4.5 The KLAC/WDC Real-World Case Study
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A common point of confusion is: "If Stock A gained $18/share and Stock B lost
$11/share, how is this a +4.69% win instead of nearly breakeven?"
The answer lies entirely in the Hedge Ratio beta (0.2970):

Trade Parameters:
- Pair: KLAC / WDC (Long KLAC, Short WDC)
- Hedge Ratio (beta): 0.2970
- Sizing Multiplier: 0.67x
- Entry Date: 2026-02-05 (Z = -2.52 sigma)
- Exit Date: 2026-02-24 (Z = -0.40 sigma, REVERSION_TARGET)
- KLAC (Stock A) Entry: $179.03 -> Exit: $196.72 (Gain: +$17.69/share)
- WDC (Stock B) Entry: $103.60 -> Exit: $113.97 (Price rose $10.37 against short)

The Arithmetic Breakdown:
- Because beta = 0.2970, for every 1.0 share of KLAC bought ($179.03), the strategy
  only shorts 0.2970 shares of WDC ($30.77).
- Total spread unit capital outlay = $179.03 + $30.77 = $209.80.
- KLAC Leg Gain = 1.0 * +$17.69 = +$17.69.
- WDC Leg Loss = 0.2970 * (-$10.37) = -$3.08 (NOT -$10.37!).
- Net Spread Profit per Unit = +$17.69 - $3.08 = +$14.61.
- Raw Spread Return = +$14.61 / $209.80 = +6.96%.
- Applying the 0.67x sizing multiplier:
    Realized Trade Net Return = +6.96% * 0.67 = +4.69% WIN.

This demonstrates why cointegrated statistical arbitrage is beta-neutral: the short
leg strictly hedges the systematic co-movement of the pair, not a 1:1 dollar amount.
